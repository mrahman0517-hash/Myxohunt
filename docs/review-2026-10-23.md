# MyxoHunt balance review

*Review of 23 Oct 2026. Method: 8 readers (code, run data, docs, tests), 4 diagnoses (ecology theory, spatial, correctness, empirical), 12 key claims each checked by a skeptical verifier against code, the run CSVs or short branch experiments (7 confirmed, 2 refuted, 3 uncertain), 3 competing plans scored by 3 judges (all three chose the fix-first plan). Claim IDs (C1–C12) and scratch evidence are in the session's review folder.*

## 1. Verdict

*Outcome (24 Oct 2026): the recommended fixes were made (B97), and the baseline of step 5 failed
the band on all 4 seeds: the prey fell to about 100–350 and the hunters shrank only slowly. The user
chose to stop tuning and document (B98).*

The goal (about 700–1,000 E. coli and about 150 Myxo from 60 bio-h on, held for 150–300+ bio-h) is probably reachable with the current rules, but nobody can say yet, because the system that ships has never been measured properly. The raw simulation is sound. What failed were the instruments. The mean-field model that picked the B86–B89 settings creates hunters out of nothing. Every comparison used one seed, and the noise between seeds (×1.3–1.6) is as large as the effects being compared. The shipped "Recommended" preset (B89 settings plus B95) was never run as a population.

The single biggest obstacle in the biology is encounters. Hunters ride slime highways and reach only a small share of the prey: they kill 15–20× less than they do with trail-following switched off. As a result the prey level is set mostly by the ground and by starvation, and the hunter level follows slowly, over 50–80 bio-h. A single 150 h run can't show either level.

## 2. What works well

The raw runs are trustworthy:
- **Repeatable:** reruns with the same seed are exact (`videos/predation_100h/stats.csv` matches `cand_B89_r8_150h` row for row).
- **Bookkeeping:** Myxo division conserves the store, every candidate run passed the efficiency overrides correctly, and `checks/prey_budget.py` is right on average.
- **Validated core:** the tick-for-tick equivalence with the paper's model still holds.

The project's own data already contain a natural experiment. The B88 and B89 ÷10 runs are identical until 24.15 bio-h, so their later split measures pure chance. The famine lysis share (B89) is the one late finding that clearly beats that noise: 250 against 93 hunters at 150 h.

## 3. Problems found, ranked by impact

### Code bugs and tooling

**1. The mean-field model makes hunters from nothing** (`meanfield/model.py:134`; confirmed).
- **The bug:** its scavenging term χ·eff·cs·starved has no H in it. At H = 0 and E = 1,000 it adds about 1.3 hunters per bio-h beyond arrivals.
- **Size:** at the model's own fixed point this term is 87% of recruitment. With it removed the hunters collapse (H at 300 h falls from 54 to 1.3).
- **A second fault:** its wake-level axis does nothing (`model.py:102-121`, `scan.py:70`). With no day of grace the death rate is 0.0288/h at every wake level.
- **Why it matters:** efficiency 60%, refill ÷8 and "losses ×0.6 gives 160–190 hunters" all came from this artefact, so treat them as unsupported.

**2. B95 switches scouting off by accident** (`myxo_modes.py:114-116`, `settings.py:164`, `settings.py:222-224`; confirmed).
- **The mechanism:** scouting is likeliest at zero prey food, which is exactly where the starvation clock runs. With `stringent_scouts=False`, a cell that has starved for 30 bio-min (3 real min at 10×) loses its spell and can't start a new one.
- **Measured:** 0.17–0.21 scouts per run (0.06% of cells) with the default, against 25 (7.9%) with the flag on. One flag-on stream gave 0.079 kills per hunter-h against 0.030; that is a hint, not yet replicated.
- **Why it matters:** scouting is the only way to find prey off the highways, and it is idle.

**3. The run tools would corrupt the next batch** (confirmed; no past run was affected).
- `checks/check_populations.py:74` turns "False" and "0" into True.
- Lines 129-134 of the same file resume an old checkpoint and silently ignore a new seed or new overrides.
- The app's founder switch (`app_params.py:147`, `web_controller.py:160-165`) takes effect at once, despite its "on Reset" label. On a running world it then drops one founder per ground update. This part rests on reading the code, not on a run.

### Design and science issues

**4. The shipped preset is unmeasured** (`settings.py:220-229`, `app_params.py:194-197`; uncertain in size). With B95 off, today's code reproduces B89 exactly, so B95 is the only change in behaviour. With it on, the first wave shrinks on seeds 1 and 2: 395 against 804 E. coli at 20 h on seed 1, and 86 against 321 at 10 h on seed 2. Seed 3 showed no shift at 10 h. Whether the 60–150 h levels move is untested.

**5. Trail lock-in limits killing** (`engine.py:61-78`, `myxo_alignment.py:110-123`; confirmed).
- **Where the cells are:** at 100 bio-h, 97% of hunter heads sit on slime. Only 8.3% of living prey lie within 2 µm of slime, against 35% of the ground (a ratio of 0.24).
- **What follows:** touches are 0.18–0.35% of hunter-ticks, giving 0.03–0.04 kills per hunter-h. Turning trail-following off raises kills ×15–20.
- **Why:** only stream leaders, scouts and cells already touching prey steer by smell, and most cells are followers.

**6. Mixed clocks** (`myxo_alignment.py:53`, `fields_update.py:41`, `settings.py:205-224`, `predation.py:19-21`; confirmed).
- **Slime:** it is laid per real tick but fades per bio-minute, so standing slime scales as 1/speed-up (4× more at 2.5× than at 10×).
- **Other processes:** scout timers run in real minutes, while kills and starvation run on the biology clock.
- **Consequences:** the B86 test ("lower speed-ups hunt worse") changed four things at once and proves nothing. The live speed-up slider silently rescales trails and scouting.

**7. Single seeds can't separate the settings** (confirmed). The ÷10 pair differed in only 2–3 cell fates by 70 h, yet its prey were ×1.62 apart at 60 h with equal hunters. That gap is chaos, not the treatment. So these comparisons are all within noise:
- refill ÷6 vs ÷10, and ÷8 vs ÷10;
- the hunting boosters;
- "lysis prevented the overshoot";
- "prey ended the same (692 vs 690)".

**8. Immortal persisters creep upward** (`ecoli_life.py:75`, `ecoli_life.py:85`; confirmed, narrowed). In prey-only runs the total stays flat at about 750, but persisters rise by about 0.9/h (71 → 195 over 80–220 h, 25% of the prey), and no dormant cell ever wakes. At refill ÷8, the ground takes 5–50 bio-days to regrow from 0.01 to the 0.2 wake level. The result is a slowly rising floor under the prey, not runaway growth. This is unverified in runs with hunters.

**9. Prey are controlled by their food supply** (partly uncertain). After 60 h, starvation deaths exceed kills 1.15–4.3× in every run since B87. These numbers are inferred from the stats files, not counted directly. Whether the prey-only plateau depends on the starting ground is untested. A rough calculation (not a simulation) suggests 1,000–1,100 from a full start.

**10. Follower smell steering** (uncertain). It gives about ×2 more kills, and about ×4 combined with other changes, in 1–2 h windows from one checkpoint. Whether the gain lasts is unknown.

### Doc inconsistencies

- `settings.py:98-100` (and B87) call `wake_threshold` 0.2 the Monod half-speed. The half-speed is actually K/(1 + 2K) = 0.2/1.4 = 0.143, so 0.2 is a chosen "real meal" level.
- B95's "scouts keep going until they find prey" fails in practice (problem 2).
- The founder switch label is wrong (problem 3).
- B86's speed-up conclusion should be marked as confounded, and B89's overshoot and "692 vs 690" remarks as chance.

### What turned out not to be a problem

Two suspected problems were tested and ruled out.
- **Hunters are not balanced on a knife-edge (C7 refuted):** a kill victim carries nearly full content (about 93 units, not 46%). A starving hunter at its core needs only 0.65× the measured kill rate to break even.
- **Hunters are regulated (C8 refuted):** the regulation is indirect, through the prey, and returns them to their level in 50–80 bio-h. B84 and B85 seed 2 show second peaks. A density-gated famine is not needed in principle.

## 4. Why the counts don't settle into the band

### The prey

Colonies keep the ground near empty. So each bio-hour the ground refills a fixed amount, which becomes about P = 21 new E. coli per bio-h however many already exist. Ecologists call this donor control, as in a chemostat.

Two things remove prey:
- mortal dormant cells die at δ = 0.69 per day ÷ 24 = 0.0288 per bio-h;
- hunters kill K cells per bio-h.

At steady state, births equal deaths:

21 = 0.0288 · E_mortal + K, so E_mortal = (21 − K) / 0.0288.

With no hunters (K = 0), E_mortal = 21 / 0.0288 ≈ 730, and prey-only runs did settle at 731–830. With K ≈ 10 per bio-h (B89 r8, 120–150 h), the level drops by 10 / 0.0288 ≈ 340. So hunters move the prey by about a third: not zero, and not all. Immortal persisters sit on top of this, adding about 0.9 per bio-h, and the large first wave leaves extra ones behind. That is why the prey sit near or above the top of the band.

### The hunters

**A well-fed hunter** holding a store of 150 burns its surplus at the full rate, 100 / 24 h = 4.17 store units per bio-h. It earns 1.91 from kills (0.036 kills × 52.8 store each) plus 1.81 from bursts of starved prey, 3.72 in total. So it loses about 0.45 per bio-h: a slow slimming, not starvation.

**A starving hunter** at its core pays 0.3 × 4.17 = 1.25 per bio-h. It breaks even at 1.25 / 52.8 = 0.024 kills per bio-h, below the measured 0.036.

So hunters are lost mainly through the famine: after 24 bio-h with no prey food within reach, 20% lyse and 10% become spores.

### The feedback

Fewer hunters mean fewer kills. That leaves more prey and more prey food, so fewer starvation clocks run and fewer cells reach the famine. This pulls the hunters back, but slowly: the fitted model's return time is 50–80 bio-h.

After a first-wave peak of 300–400 hunters at about 85 h, settling takes one or two return times. At 140 h, 90–100% of the B88 and B89 hunters were still starving and declining. The model expects B89 r8 to reach about 120 hunters by 300 h (untested). On top of this, seeds differ ×1.3–1.6. A single 150 h run therefore shows a transient seen through noise, and the band can't be judged from it.

## 5. Recommended route

Measure what ships, then move one lever. This is plan_fixfirst with the judges' additions.

1. **Fix tooling and docs:** boolean parsing, a resume that refuses a changed seed, atomic checkpoints, the founder switch and the wake-level comment.
   - *Check:* 3 new tests; all 89 existing tests still pass.
   - *Compute:* about 2 min.
2. **Add direct counters and an energy ledger.**
   - *Counters:* births, kills, starvation deaths, wakes, persisters, famine fates, scout-minutes, touches, killed-prey content, starvation-clock resets, and absorbed food by source (kills, starved bursts and scraps separately).
   - *Ledger:* a closed-world test that balances to 1e-6.
   - *Check:* the counters match `prey_budget.py` within Poisson error over 10 bio-h.
   - *Compute:* about 15 min.
3. **Measure the machine.** Time 1, 2 and 4 parallel branches, and profile one bio-hour. Speed up only code outside the validated core; `relax_springs` and the equivalence tests stay exact.
   - *Compute:* about 20 min.
4. **Settle clocks and scouting** (your decisions D1 and D2 below). Check the scout fix in branches from `predation_100h.ckpt`, 3 streams × 6 bio-h per arm.
   - *Target:* scouts at 3–10% of cells.
   - *Compute:* about 30 min wall on 4 slots.
5. **Run the baseline overnight:** Recommended plus the fixes, seeds 1–4, to 300 bio-h.
   - *Early stop:* abort any seed that can't pass (H < 45 or E > 2,000 after 100 h).
   - *Alongside:* prey only from a full start, seeds 1–2, to 250 bio-h, to settle whether the plateau depends on the start.
   - *Optional:* 2 seeds started near the target on depleted ground for 150 h, to skip the first wave.
   - *Gate:* if the baseline passes, stop and document it.
   - *Compute:* about 20–25 CPU-h, 6–9 h wall.
6. **Find the hunter balance point directly.**
   - *Branches:* from the 150 h snapshots, thin only the hunters (to 75, 150 and 300), so the prey's layout stays intact. Run 3 streams × 20 bio-h per level. The level where per-hunter growth crosses zero is the equilibrium, and the slope gives the return time.
   - *Model check before more runs:* rebuild the reduced model with scavenging × H/(H + H½), the measured energetics (93-unit kills, 31-unit bursts, upkeep 4.17 and 1.25 per bio-h) and a 4-stage famine delay. Use it only to answer one question, which takes seconds: does any backed setting give 750–950 prey and 130–170 hunters?
   - *Compute:* about 9 CPU-h, 2–3 h wall.
7. **One lever, one round, then you decide** (D3). If persisters exceed 20% of the prey by 150 h, offer the slow persister death (D4).
8. **Confirm and record:** 4 seeds × 300 bio-h, then update the decision log, `parameters.md`, `sources.md` and the preset.
   - *Compute:* about 20 CPU-h.

**Pass rule.** Each seed is judged on 150–300 bio-h, and at least 3 of 4 seeds must pass:
- means of 700–1,000 E. coli and 100–220 Myxo;
- at least 80% of hourly samples within 500–1,300 E. coli and 75–300 Myxo;
- minima of at least 210 E. coli and 45 Myxo;
- drift under 20% of the mean per 100 bio-h.

**Total:** about 45–50 CPU-h over 2–3 days, or about 25 CPU-h if the step-5 gate passes.

### Alternatives

- **(A) Ship now with honest caveats.** Lock the clocks, apply the scout fix and run the 4-seed baseline once, but move no lever.
  - *For:* the cheapest route (about 25 CPU-h), and in line with "deliver first".
  - *Against:* the band may be missed.
- **(B) Model-guided calibration (plan_calibrate).** Measure rates in thinned branches, place the equilibrium analytically, then confirm (about 37 CPU-h).
  - *For:* the most rigorous on paper.
  - *Against:* thinning the prey breaks the highway layout that controls kills, and its checkpoints are still transient. It also trusts a fitted model again, which is how the last round went wrong.
- **(C) New mechanisms (plan_mechanism):** a quorum-gated famine (Kuspa, Plamann & Kaiser 1992) and spore germination.
  - *For:* real biology.
  - *Against:* it solves a problem the data refute (C8). It adds about 5 free parameters, its 2D density sensor already misfired in the B84 crowding check, and germination has no source yet.
  - *Best use:* a fallback if the hunters go extinct.

## 6. What not to do

- **Don't rank settings on one seed,** or even on 4-seed means. Rates come from paired branches, and levels from several seeds.
- **Don't let a fitted average model choose settings.** Use it for direction and return times only, and check its terms for anything that creates cells from nothing.
- **Don't compare speed-ups** until each process has one declared clock.
- **Don't read 60–150 h means as equilibria** while 90% of the hunters are still starving.
- **Don't add mechanisms** before the shipped system is measured.
- **Don't use "within 10 µm" as contact;** count touches.
- **Cap each tuning round:** one lever, one confirmation, then your decision. Every round so far opened a new question while the deliverables waited.

## 7. Decisions for you

**D1. The scouting block**
- (a) Block scouting only after `behaviour_delay`, 2 bio-h of starving. This keeps B95's intent (Harris 1998: starvation recognition). **(recommended)**
- (b) `stringent_scouts=True`: about 8% of cells scout, a large change in behaviour.
- (c) Keep the block.

**D2. Clocks**
- (a) Fix the speed-up at 10× and lock or label the slider. Nothing changes in validated runs, and no reruns are needed. **(recommended)**
- (b) Fade slime on the real clock (36 real min). This is principled, since slime is physical, and identical at 10×.
- (c) Scale the slime deposit by speed-up/10, and move the scout timers to bio-minutes.

**D3. Hunter lever, if one is needed**
- (a) The famine lysis share. Its value is disputed: classic counts say 60–80% lyse, while O'Connor & Zusman 1988 found at least 80% survive. It already sits at 20%, so it can only reduce the hunters. **(recommended if there are too many hunters)**
- (b) The famine time: 24 bio-h now (Lee 2012), and it can move within the measured range.
- (c) The starving cost, 0.3 now (assumed). It is the natural lever if there are too few hunters.

**D4. Persisters**
- (a) Keep them immortal and measure the drift. **(recommended until the baseline shows persisters above 20% of the prey)**
- (b) Let them die slowly, at δ/10 or δ/3. Finkel 2006 describes long-term survivors as a "dynamic" population; the rate is our assumption.

**D5. Route**
- The recommended route above. **(recommended)**
- (A) Ship now.
- (B) Calibrate with a rebuilt model.
- (C) Add the new mechanisms.

**D6. Look criteria.** Proposed: at least 10 rippling cells, at least 2% of hunters in a killing pause, at most 60% of hunters starving, and scouts at 3–10% of cells at zero prey food.
- (a) Make them pass criteria.
- (b) Report them but don't pass or fail on them. Trail lock-in may keep kills a minority loss, so the counts could pass while the hunting looks weak. **(recommended)**
